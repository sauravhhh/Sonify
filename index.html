Hear a picture. Send it as sound.

Load any image and play it as sound — the picture is scanned like a spectrogram: high rows ring out as high notes, low rows rumble low, and the brighter an area, the louder it sings. Or go further: transmit the picture itself as a stream of tones, and rebuild it on the other side from sound alone — through the air, or from an audio file.

The image is read left to right, one column at a time. In each column, every row becomes a note — top rows are high pitches, bottom rows are low — and the brighter the pixel, the louder its note. A face, a letter, a skyline: each has its own chord sweep.

Send the image as sound

A different method: the picture is shrunk to a small grid and every pixel is turned into one short tone — bright pixels beep high, dark pixels beep low, row by row, with a marker beep starting each row. Play it out loud and a phone running Receive sound can rebuild the picture from the tones alone. No file ever changes hands — just sound.

Receive an image from sound

Point this device at a speaker playing a Sonify transmission, or feed it the transmission as an audio file. It listens for the marker beeps, measures every tone's pitch, and rebuilds the picture pixel by pixel. For the clearest copy, keep the volume up and the room quiet.
